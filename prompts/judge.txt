Rules for "reveals":
- A fact is revealed if a person who knows the fact, answering the question truthfully and directly, would state the fact or make it immediately obvious.
- Sharing a topic is not enough. "What are your finances like?" does not reveal "the landlord refused to install a charger".
- A very broad question ("Are there any other risks?") reveals a fact only if that fact would clearly be the first thing a reasonable person mentions in reply.
- A question can reveal zero, one, or several facts.
- Judge each fact independently. Do not try to guess which facts matter for the decision.

Rule for "generic": mark a question generic if it would make sense, word for word, for a clearly unrelated decision (for example "What are your long-term goals?" or "Have you considered the risks?").
